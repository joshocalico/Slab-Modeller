# Block out a room

We will hollow a 4 m by 3 m by 4 m box from the top. The box becomes walls, a floor, and an open top.

<Figure
  src="/img/solid-box.png"
  alt="A closed blue box solid in a 3D view."
  caption="Object mode, one SlabSolid. The shots use a 4 m by 3 m by 4 m box."
/>

## Prepare the editor

If you opened the full Slab source checkout, open `project/` in Godot 4.7, then open or create a 3D scene. If you copied Slab into another project, enable the plugin and open or create a 3D scene with a `Node3D` root.

Open the **Slab** bottom panel. With the 3D view focused, press <kbd>F</kbd> to frame the selected solid. The mouse wheel zooms, and Godot's numpad view shortcuts change the viewing direction.

## Create the first solid

1. Press <kbd>1</kbd> for Object mode.
2. Choose **Box** in **Create**.
3. Set **Width** to 4, **Height** to 3, and **Depth** to 4.
4. Click **Create**.
5. Move the gold preview over the world ground plane, then click to place it. You can also press Enter.

A 4 m by 3 m by 4 m `SlabSolid` appears where you placed it. Press Escape or right-click if you need to cancel and start again.

Press <kbd>4</kbd> for Face mode. You should see a closed rectangular volume that matches the shot.

## Inset the top

1. Click the top face.
2. In **Face**, click **Inset**, or press <kbd>I</kbd>.
3. Set **Inset distance** to 0.45.
4. Inspect the preview. The original solid has not changed yet.
5. Click **Apply**, or press Enter.

The top face shrinks in place. A rim of new faces appears around it. The inner cap stays selected.

<Figure
  src="/img/solid-inset.png"
  alt="The same box after the top face was inset, showing a smaller inner cap and a rim."
  caption="After Inset. The inner cap stays selected on the top plane."
/>

## Extrude the inner face down

1. Keep the inner cap selected.
2. In **Face**, click **Extrude**, or press <kbd>E</kbd>.
3. Set **Extrude distance** to -2.4.
4. Inspect the preview. The cap should stop above the bottom face and leave a floor.
5. Click **Apply**, or press Enter.

The negative distance moves inward. Extrude keeps the inset rim and builds one inner wall loop down to the new cap.

You should see a floor and four inner walls. The inset and extrude are separate undo actions.

If a preview is wrong, click **Cancel**, press Escape, or right-click. Slab leaves the solid unchanged.

/>

## Cut a door

1. Press <kbd>1</kbd>.
2. Choose **Box** in **Create**. Set **Width** to 1, **Height** to 2, and **Depth** to 1.
3. Click **Create**, move the gold preview near the wall, then click to place it. That solid is the cutter.
4. Keep both nodes under the same parent. This exact placement assumes the room has no rotation or scale. In the cutter's Inspector, open **Transform > Position** and set it to the room's Position plus `(1.5, 0.6, -0.2)`.
5. Inspect the negative-Z wall. The cutter should start on the room floor and project through both wall surfaces.
6. Click the room solid, then Shift-click the cutter. Confirm the panel names the room **Target** and the cutter **Second solid**; use **Swap operands** if needed.
7. In **Object**, click **Subtract**.

Slab knifes both wall faces and joins the hole with the cutter's sides. Delete or hide the cutter node afterward.

<Figure
  src="/img/solid-door.png"
  alt="The hollow room with a doorway cut through one wall."
  caption="Subtract through a wall. The opening stays a closed solid."
/>

If you want a softer top corner, bevel those edges before you inset. **Bevel** can use 1 to 16 segments. After the volume is hollow, bevel on those rim edges is often refused. To make an arch, switch to Object mode, choose **Arch** in **Create**, click **Create**, then place the preview.
