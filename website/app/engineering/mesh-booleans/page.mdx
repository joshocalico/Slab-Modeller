# Mesh booleans

Godot CSG is a live node tree. `CSGBox3D` under `CSGCombiner3D` rebuilds a mesh from the inspector. That is a different job from clicking two `SlabSolid` nodes and subtracting.

Slab's Subtract, Union, and Intersect run on two closed half-edge meshes. The first selected solid is the host. The second is the cutter. The result has to stay a volume. If the op would leave a sheet, a hole that does not punch through, or a disjoint leftover, Slab reports a fault and keeps the old mesh.

## Why a leak is refused

A blockout wall is a thin box. A doorway is a cutter that sticks out both sides. The kernel knifes both wall faces and joins the hole. A cutter that only nicks one side would open the volume. The editor would then draw a one-sided wall, and the next extrude would have no twin to walk.

Zhou, Grinspun, Zorin, and Jacobson's mesh-arrangement work is the current graphics treatment of exact-ish solid booleans on triangle meshes. Slab is smaller. It does not claim their robustness. The product rule is the same shape: a boolean is legal when the output is still a solid.

## What to read first

If you only open one paper, open the 2016 mesh arrangements paper. Then read Guibas and Stolfi so the half-edge walk in [Half-edge meshes](/engineering/half-edge-meshes) is not a slogan.

For the in-editor steps, see [Punch a cavity](/guide/how-to/punch-a-cavity).
